# Experiment Log: Iteration V2 — Polyvinyl Acetate (PVAc) in Acetone

---

## 1. Objective & Hypothesis
- **Objective:** Evaluate pure PVAc dissolved in acetone as a ductile replacement for brittle polystyrene.
- **Hypothesis:** Because PVAc has a significantly lower glass transition temperature ($T_g \approx 30^\circ\text{C}$) and flexible ester side-chains, in-flight flash spinning will yield flexible, leathery, or rubbery strands that can span a gap and sustain mechanical tension without crumbling into dust.
- **Primary Variables Under Test:**
  1. PVAc extraction efficiency and dissolution rate in acetone.
  2. Concentration curve ($7.5\text{ wt\%}$, $12.5\text{ wt\%}$, $17.5\text{ wt\%}$, $22.5\text{ wt\%}$) to map the droplet $\rightarrow$ beads-on-string $\rightarrow$ cohesive fiber transition.

---

## 2. Immediate Pre-Flight Checklist: PVAc Extraction
Follow [EXTRACTION_PROTOCOLS.md](../EXTRACTION_PROTOCOLS.md):
- [ ] Spread a thin film ($0.2\text{–}0.5\text{ mm}$) of white glue onto silicone or smooth aluminum foil.
- [ ] Blast with hairdryer on medium heat until the film turns completely transparent (3–5 min).
- [ ] Peel the transparent PVAc sheet.
- [ ] Cut sheet into tiny confetti strips ($2\text{ mm} \times 5\text{ mm}$).
- [ ] Weigh out batches:
  - Batch A ($7.5\text{ wt\%}$): $1.5\text{ g}$ PVAc + $18.5\text{ g}$ ($23.6\text{ mL}$) Acetone
  - Batch B ($12.5\text{ wt\%}$): $2.5\text{ g}$ PVAc + $17.5\text{ g}$ ($22.3\text{ mL}$) Acetone
  - Batch C ($17.5\text{ wt\%}$): $3.5\text{ g}$ PVAc + $16.5\text{ g}$ ($21.0\text{ mL}$) Acetone
  - Batch D ($22.5\text{ wt\%}$): $4.5\text{ g}$ PVAc + $15.5\text{ g}$ ($19.8\text{ mL}$) Acetone
- [ ] Seal in glass jars and shake intermittently until completely dissolved. *(Note: Confetti should dissolve in ~5–10 minutes. If it appears milky or forms white globs, residual moisture is present).*
